4 · Next: overhauling the assessment builder

The builder works, but editing one question means jumping between four separate tabs, there's no way to test an assessment before publishing it, and you only find out something's wrong after you hit publish. I researched how the leading assessment platforms solve this and turned it into a plan.

Researched against Qualtrics, Culture Amp, Typeform, ScoreApp and others · a phased, low-risk plan

What the research said

The best tools let you edit a question in one place and validate it by running it — not by imagining it across tabs. On the things that make our tool special (versioning, comparability across rounds), we're actually ahead of the field; we just hide it.

The plan, sequenced for value-first, low risk

1

Test before you publish

Fill in sample answers and instantly see the scores, tiers, and recommendations a respondent would get — writing nothing. The single highest-value addition.

2

"Safe to publish" checklist

Surface any problems before you publish, as a live checklist, instead of an error after the fact.

3

One-screen editor

Then, only if it earns its keep, bring everything about a question onto one screen — done last, and reversible.

Why this order

An independent engineering review recommended shipping the two high-value capabilities first — they help regardless of layout — and treating the bigger visual rebuild as the last, optional step. That's a deliberately conservative sequence: value early, risk late.
